# Baseline-supported copying-head follow-up: prospective protocol

## Question and scope

The output-dose study (PR #636) did not meet its H2-versus-H10 rule: 5/8 family contrasts were positive, and only 3/8 repeated prompts had the target as the unique clean top token. This follow-up asks whether that head contrast is present on disjoint public families whose clean next-token copying is supported on two different repeated orders, while retaining and intervening on the baseline-unsupported held-out families too.

This is a public, exploratory fixed-list experiment, chosen after earlier results. It is not blind to earlier results, representative of language generally, or evidence of absence from pretraining. The first block in every shuffle contains the target; this comparison does not isolate a unique copying mechanism. The previous studies remain unchanged.

## Baseline screen

Run the 192 fixed prompts once, retaining all 50,257 float32 logits and their row identities. For each of the two rotations in each family, the repeated target must be the unique full-vocabulary top token, and its clean target log probability must be at least 0.5 natural-log units above each of its three corresponding shuffle prompts. Ties fail unique-top eligibility. Use full-vocabulary log-softmax with a maximum shift and stable binary64 summation. Compute each gap directly with math.fsum of the two target logits, two maxima and two shifted log-normalizers, avoiding subtraction of separately rounded log probabilities. Report the repeated and shuffled top IDs and tie counts, target ranks and probabilities, every six repeat-minus-shuffle gap, and the exact reasons a family fails. No cutoff is changed after the screen. Finite valid rows that do not pass are baseline-unsupported, not failed edits. Missing/nonfinite rows or execution/cleanup uncertainty invalidate the attempt rather than silently counting as a baseline failure.

The held-out families are baseline-screened: their clean predictions are observed before intervention, but no new intervention output is used for selection. They are held out from earlier interventions and from the development family set, not from the clean baseline or model pretraining. A single next-token top for each of two orders is not a guarantee of multi-token generation or stability to other wordings.

## Intervention and analysis

Run all 12 held-out families, including baseline-unsupported ones, whenever their fixed dose plans are valid; do not fill, replace or reorder families based on baseline or edited results. This preserves descriptive contrasts for unsupported baselines even when fewer than six supported families make the primary criterion ineligible. For each family, run the eight clean/no-op pairs first, requiring each clean full-logit row to equal its published screen row bitwise, each no-op logit row to equal that clean row, and each no-op's complete projection input before and after the hook to be bitwise equal. The unhooked screen has no projection-input witness. Each edited pre-input must equal its own prompt's no-op pre-input bitwise; every untouched input coordinate and every other token position of the projection output must match the corresponding no-op. Clean-after logits must equal the clean row bitwise. Then compute one family dose from those same eight no-op inputs: for each H2, H10 and H7 at layer 3 final input position 10, let g[v,h] = x[v,h] @ W_h using the checkpoint's 64 input rows of c_proj in binary64 with math.fsum; q_out is the minimum norm over all 24 pairs. H7 is one fixed same-layer active control. Use the output-dose study's 2^-8 floor, float32 planned-bit rounding, and 1e-4 times q_out relative tolerance for each predicted and actual norm, 24-way range and actual-versus-predicted output vector. If a finite correctly landed plan misses dose tolerance, mark that family dose-ineligible; a wrong bit, unexplained witness discrepancy, nonfinite record or uncertain cleanup invalidates the attempt. Retain all attempted outputs and still execute every preplanned arm after a finite measured mismatch. A floor or predicted-dose failure skips active arms for that family, with clean-after retained.

For each prompt run the three eligible active arms in a fixed rotation of (H2,H10,H7) by (8*(family-1)+variant) modulo 3, followed by clean-after. Compare each arm to its corresponding clean row. Save full logits, full projection before/after/input and output witnesses, exact planned and landed bits, input removals, actual output vectors/norms and pairwise cosines; report target logits, log probabilities, ranks, unique tops/ties, directional KL(clean||arm), total variation and top changes. Matching an output norm does not match direction, removed input norm or downstream nonlinear gain. The controlling head/variant and q_out are disclosed for every family.

For each rotation and head, D is the change in target log probability on the repeated prompt minus the mean change across that rotation's three shuffles. For each family and head, D is the mean of its two rotation Ds; C = D_H10 - D_H2. The primary population is held-out families that pass both the fixed baseline screen and the family output-dose checks. The prespecified criterion is met only with at least six primary families, strictly positive mean C and C > 0 in at least 75% of those families. If six or more are eligible and the criterion fails, report criterion-not-met; with fewer than six report ineligible. Report all held-out dose-eligible families, baseline-supported and unsupported strata, both rotation-level contrasts, and H7 comparisons separately as descriptive. The fixed rule is not a significance test or evidence of a unique mechanism.
