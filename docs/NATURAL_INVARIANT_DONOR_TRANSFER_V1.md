# Prospective invariant-noun donor transfer, v1

**Status: predeclared public protocol.** No transfer patch in this study was forwarded before this protocol was published. This separate public experiment asks whether an already selected L3-final attention-output site can transfer a matched donor's output behavior between naturally different prompts. The site and model were selected using earlier exposed studies. The v40 invariant-noun inputs are public and already reserved; their clean/no-op values and the separate study's intervention outcomes may be observed or published before this study runs. Our evaluation partition is held out only from this study's discovery-derived selection and future transfer-patch effects. It is not blind to the prompts, prior baseline exposure, pretraining, or the broader research record. We do not access or retry the old protected split.

This is donor-consistent transfer, not necessarily grammatical rescue. Patching a `this sheep` receiver from `these sheep` may make the receiver's own correct prediction worse. It does not isolate an abstract number feature: determiner identity, context, semantic and lexical variation, and nonlinear gain remain live alternatives. The four publicly named candidate interventions all occur at the same preselected site; this is not a new site-localization test.

## Four fixed candidates and controls

Let `B` be the receiver's saved L3-final float32 vector and `C` the same-noun opposite-determiner donor vector. Let `d = binary64(C) − binary64(B)` coordinatewise and `q = sqrt(fsum(d_i*d_i))`. Check finite inputs and require `q > 2^-20`. Candidate IDs are fixed and public for all cases:

| Candidate | Patch at the receiver's L3-final attention output | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Scaled difference from the same-determiner, other-noun donor | Active lexical-substitution control |
| Q2 | Exact same-noun, opposite-determiner donor `C` | Natural donor transfer |
| Q3 | One-channel cyclic shift of `d`, added to `B` | Single active rotated-direction control |
| Q4 | Scaled difference from the opposite-determiner, other-noun donor | Noun-matching control at fixed donor determiner |

Q1 and Q4 answer different questions; neither is a feature-free null. For each scaled other-noun control form `v = binary64(C_control) − binary64(B)`, require its norm finite and greater than `2^-20`, compute binary64 `s=q/||v||`, then `h_i=binary64(s*v_i)`. For Q3 let `h_i=d_(i−1 mod 768)`. Form `z_i=round_binary32_RNE(binary64(B_i)+h_i)` with a checked nearest-even thread mode; Q2 lands the original `C` bits. The input norm of each non-self control must agree with `q` within relative `2e-4`, and its full realized displacement must agree with `h` within `2e-4*q`. Also require the intended `h` norm within relative `1e-12` of q. Perform the squared comparisons using exact rational values of the recorded binary32/binary64 operands, rather than rounded square roots. Nonfinite/overflow/zero vectors, undelivered patches or a failed certificate cause a retained fail-stop, not rescaling, redraw, exclusion or a silent retry. Equal input L2 does not equalize functional effect or make the rotated control a randomization distribution.

For each action retain the whole L3 module output immediately before/after the patch, planned and landed float32 vectors, all untouched-bit comparisons, the intended binary64 displacement and exact norm/vector certificates, donor and receiver case IDs and hashes, and full last-position vocabulary logits. Compare Q2's landing exactly with `C`; compare each pre-patch vector with saved `B`. All other positions and channels outside the replacement must be bit-identical. The original saved no-op/clean-repeat controls remain documented, but do not attest new-process execution.

## Discovery selection and predictions

Let `M = logit(318) − logit(389)` at the final position and `delta_q = M(patch_q) − M(receiver baseline)`. For a `these` receiver define orientation `s=+1`; for `this`, `s=−1`. This fixed orientation is based on the receiver's public determiner and expects movement toward its opposite-determiner donor, independently of observed scores. Feed a separate function only the 24 numerically ordered rows of `(s*delta_Q1, ..., s*delta_Q4)` from discovery; do not pass case IDs, nouns, roles or coordinates. Rank candidates by mean oriented effect, highest first, breaking exact ties by Q1–Q4 order; select the first only if its mean is strictly positive, otherwise abstain. This is a role-blind function interface with a public fixed Q mapping, not human blinding; the direct donor is always Q2, so a constant-Q2 rule identifies the publicly named direct-donor candidate in 24/24 receivers by construction, before observing effects. A Q2 win compares direct transfer with the active controls; it does not establish that the selector discovered a site. A control or an overshooting patch can win and must be reported as such.

Before any evaluation patch, compute and publicly freeze all four rankings, ties, the selection/abstention, and the four mean oriented discovery effects. For each evaluation receiver, the fixed signed prediction for Q is `s * mean_discovery_oriented_effect_Q`. The discovery mean is the proposed prediction method, not an additional comparator; no separate pooled-mean comparator is claimed. For every Q compare the 24 absolute prediction errors on the same cases with both zero and the fixed full-output-transfer forecast `M(same_noun_opposite_determiner_donor) − M(receiver baseline)`, using the already retained clean/no-op logits. This donor forecast is a separate comparator, not a fitted weight or a claim that the patch transfers the whole output. Report its signed value and error for every Q, plus every target's signed effect, discovery-mean prediction and error, zero error, both determiner directions and selected candidate. Report each comparator's MAE; a strict lower MAE beats a comparator and equality does not. If discovery abstains, its selected-candidate evaluation is ineligible; do not select another after observing evaluation.

## Primary evaluation and negative results

For every evaluation receiver let `D0=||logits_receiver − logits_same_noun_opposite_determiner_donor||₂` and `Dq=||logits_patch_q − logits_donor||₂` over the entire last-position vocabulary. For each Q report `R_q = 1 − mean(Dq)/mean(D0)` across all 24 receivers; retain each per-case `1−Dq/D0` and its undefined status. These are distances toward an opposite-determiner donor output, not probability of correctness or a percentage of causal pathway restored. Let baseline adequacy require at least 18/24 receiver baselines to have `M>0` for `this` and `M<0` for `these`, and at least 9/12 matched this/these pairs to satisfy `M(this)>M(these)`; exact zero is not correct. The aggregate denominator is defined only when mean D0 exceeds `1e-12`.

If all planned actions and certificates are valid, baseline adequacy holds and mean D0 is defined, the fixed primary descriptive pattern is supported only if both `R_Q2 > 0.25` and `R_Q2 − max(R_Q1,R_Q3,R_Q4) > 0.05`, with strict comparisons. Otherwise it is unsupported; if the baseline gate, denominator or required evidence is invalid, it is ineligible, with each continuous result retained. A supported or unsupported conclusion is conditional on the retained receiver and donor baseline logits applying in the new process; the pre-patch vector equality alone cannot establish that condition or attribute all output differences to the patch. These numerical cutoffs are operational, informed by the earlier artificial-repair experiment and not calibrated significance levels. Report baseline and patched recipient-correct sign rates separately, regardless of primary outcome. Do not remove poorly performing cases, conflate an abstention with zero effect, average signed directions before orientation, or call a negative result proof of equivalence.

## Schedule, bounds and chronology

The fixed schedule is Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4 for each of 24 discovery receivers, then the same order for 24 evaluation receivers: 96 new patch forwards per phase, 192 total. Prior saved baseline and controls are read, not forwarded again. Publish this protocol, fixed mapping, exact source and admission/receipt bindings before discovery patches. After the closed discovery attempt and independent saved-byte review, publish and independently read back the freeze before any evaluation patch; the source verifies the local freeze matches the saved discovery evidence. Public-readback chronology is an external observation, not established by a local file hash alone.

Use an existing worktree and put all new output, temporary data, bytecode prefixes and checkpoint staging on E:. A phase is bounded to 64 MiB raw tensor evidence plus 8 MiB metadata, with the exact per-action worst-case reserve checked before every forward; require at least 2 GiB free on E: before model loading, at least 1 GiB plus the next-action reserve during execution, and 256 MiB on each source/cache volume. A direct-child receipt records exit, interruption, hashes and empty staging/cache-prefix observations; these are cooperative observations, not authenticated execution or descendant attestation. An absent receipt or uncertain child state is incomplete and must be reconciled before any retry.

The 24 receivers per partition reuse two nouns across two frames and three contexts; they are correlated. The three controls and 24 cases are not independent site trials or a population-level test. Preserve exact raw logits, all controls, no-effect cases, any incomplete phase and all negative values.
