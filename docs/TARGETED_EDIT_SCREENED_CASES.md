The target strata have eight prompts each. `s1` uses direct national, official, present, or current-capital statements; `s2` uses country, national, or Republic possessive and listing formulations; `s3` explicitly names or designates the capital city; and `s4` uses answer, question, map, list, fill-in, or country-context formulations. The ordered rows in the JSON define the frozen within-stratum order. These closely related prompts are not independent samples. Each asks for France's national capital; Paris is correct and London is the intended counterfactual token. The 12 locality prompts comprise four other-country capitals, four non-capital France-related facts, and four unrelated facts. Locality expected-token metrics describe changes on these prompts, including any prompt the baseline gets wrong; they are not a general factual-accuracy or capability test.

## Interpreting an approximate match

The fixed matched-arm gates do not establish equal target effects. As a fabricated illustration, suppose both discovery gains are +1.2, all eight target baselines are −0.5, and projected and unprojected gains are +0.9 and +1.0 respectively. All eight reverse and the evaluated mean-gap rule passes. Suppose each of 12 binary locality rows has baseline logits `[2, 0]`, projected logits `[2, 0.09]`, and unprojected logits `[2, 0.10]`. Their respective locality KLs are approximately 0.000435 and 0.000538; the top token is unchanged, and projected expected-top NLL worsens by about 0.0112 nats. Those locality checks pass even though the projected locality-logit shift is 10% smaller. With the other target/control requirements also met, the matched arm could pass without excluding reduced perturbation strength as an explanation of its lower KL. This example is a calculation, not a model result. Report actual discovery and per-target gains and each locality row; describe a lower KL as an observation in the fixed approximate comparison, not proof of superior preservation at equal strength.
