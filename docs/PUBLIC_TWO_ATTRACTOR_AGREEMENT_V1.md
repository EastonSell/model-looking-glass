# Public two-attractor number agreement evaluation

## Question and fixed design

Does the number of a second intervening noun combine additively with the first noun's number in DistilGPT-2's preference for an agreeing verb? This is a public, prospective evaluation using a fixed checkpoint and prompts selected without consulting model outputs. It is complementary to the project's single-attractor subject-dependence study, not a replication of its causal interventions. Two-attractor agreement has been studied previously; see [Arehalli and Linzen (2020)](https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/cogsci20/papers/0069/index.html) and [Zacharopoulos et al. (2023)](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.998/).

The accompanying case file SHA-256 is `fcb79a2762a2fd9fa346347c5156f5eb87bba314b1c8a021fe5fb639827f2798`. Its 128 inputs use four subject lemmas (key, chair, lamp, coin), two intervening-noun pairs (cabinet/truck, window/bench), both orders of each pair, both subject numbers, and all four combinations of intervening-noun numbers. The only template is `The SUBJECT near the NOUN beside the NOUN`. All cells are kept: there is no outcome-based exclusion, no tuning, and no replacement of failed cases. The subject and each possible ` is`, ` are`, ` was`, ` were` continuation are single tokens under the pinned tokenizer. The same ordered inputs and contrasts apply regardless of the results.

For a lexical holdout fixed before any of this study's model forwards, the 32 cases with subject pairs 3–4 and noun pair 2 are the evaluation subset. The 32 cases with subject pairs 1–2 and noun pair 1 are the development-labeled subset. The remaining 32-case groups are the new-attractor-pair-only subset (subject pairs 1–2 and noun pair 2) and the new-subject-only subset (subject pairs 3–4 and noun pair 1). No decision or selection is conditioned on development results; all 128 inputs and all analyses are fixed together. These are held out from this study's development and known project exposures, not from model pretraining. All groups use the same syntactic template; the evaluation subset is not a held-out syntax test.

## Measurements

The outcome is the final-position logit difference `m = logit(" is") - logit(" are")` (token IDs 318 and 389). Its subject-signed version `M` is `m` for a singular subject and `-m` for a plural subject. Positive `M` favors the number matching the subject; a zero margin is counted as incorrect. For each fixed subject lemma, subject number, noun pair and noun order, let `M00`, `M10`, `M01`, `M11` denote whether the near and beside nouns respectively match (0) or mismatch (1) the subject number. All-congruent `M00` is the matched baseline. Prespecified per-unit quantities are:

- near-noun shift: `((M10 - M00) + (M11 - M01)) / 2`;
- beside-noun shift: `((M01 - M00) + (M11 - M10)) / 2`;
- interaction: `M11 - M10 - M01 + M00`.

The primary descriptive value is the mean interaction over all 32 units. Zero is the additive reference on this logit-margin scale; no equivalence or population claim follows from a small value. Report its evaluation-subset mean, each other lexical partition, each subject number, subject lemma, noun pair and order, and all per-unit quantities. Also report all four raw continuation logits and both margins per case, condition means and accuracy counts. Report all-congruent baseline counts by partition and subject number: correct, ties, wrong-sign margins, and incorrect including ties. As a behavioral control, for each fixed subject lemma, noun pair, noun order and absolute noun-number cell, compare raw `m` for the singular subject minus raw `m` for its plural form; report all 64 such shifts and their partition-by-cell means. This evaluates subject sensitivity without proving a syntactic mechanism. In each unit, the raw-number interaction `m(SS)-m(SP)-m(PS)+m(PP)` is also retained. The same calculations for `was` versus `were` (IDs 373 and 547) are secondary. No significance test, case selection or success threshold is declared.

## Execution and limits

Use `distilbert/distilgpt2` revision `2290a62682d06624634c1f46a6ad5be0f47f38aa`, model weights SHA-256 `e1ff18884359fe8beb795a5f414feb85a6ce3d929ad019c0d958c039d2b94a1b`, tokenizer SHA-256 `8414cab924d8b9b33013f0d221c5862f365ee9be39c5c2bfae8a5a9e970478a6`, offline CPU float32, eager attention, no gradients or generation, no padding or truncation, and `use_cache=False`. The runner verifies its cases and tokenizer and appends four logits for each attempted input before the next input. Retain partial output and the actual process exit on failure; do not represent an incomplete run as a result.

Before forwarding, publish these cases and runner, obtain the existing shared inventory's reviewed planned-input reservation, and recheck the latest recorded public exposure and other fixed planned inputs. Public full-row-prefix checks against the original natural study's 128-row sampler support and the successor's 256 public pool protect those studies without opening their sealed records. The original-support conclusion is conditional on the original preparation following its published sampler and tokenizer. Unknown prior project exposure and model pretraining are not ruled out. No protected split, nonce, or original claim is used or changed by this study.

This is a descriptive test of one checkpoint, four subjects, two noun pairs and one template. The second phrase may attach to the first noun or to the subject phrase; changing noun number changes the text, so any shift is an association in this design, not proof of a particular mechanism or syntactic parse. Position and lexical identity are counterbalanced but distance and phrase type are not independently manipulated. This evaluation neither proves a new general phenomenon nor estimates population performance.
