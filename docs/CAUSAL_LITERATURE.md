# Causal interventions: evidence and experimental choices

Status: the literature review and proposed methods below were written on 2026-10-28. The papers provide motivation and warnings, not proof that the methods work on this project's models or tasks. The consolidated [roadmap and execution-efficiency plan](RESEARCH_ROADMAP.md) is a living planning document, and the original synthetic CPU measurements are in [RESULTS.md](../RESULTS.md).

Subsequent project work has produced small **pretrained development studies**, with important limits. In a [DistilBERT agreement study](../results/bert-agreement-supervised-dev-v1-20261028/README.md), the mean signed contrast after subject-state patching moved in the donor-number direction on one public development family, but an earlier-layer comparison produced a larger average shift; this does not identify a unique number computation. A [DistilGPT2 agreement preflight](../results/distilgpt2-agreement-preflight-v1-20261028/README.md) recorded 16 correct next-token preferences on public prompts without testing an internal explanation. A [copying screen](../results/distilgpt2-copying-dev-v1-20261028/README.md) observed a continuation difference relative to first-block shuffles and proposed an attention head for intervention; the shuffles also change local transitions, and the screen alone does not establish that the head causes the behavior. A [targeted-edit study](../results/targeted-edit-cpu-v1-20261028/README.md) produced a large counterfactual target shift but failed its prespecified locality criteria. These outcomes are conditional on the stated prompts, interventions and metrics; they do not establish broad generalization, model reasoning, or preserved capabilities. See the linked records for controls, raw evidence, and exposure limits.

Later results sharpen those limits. The [balanced DistilBERT evaluation](../results/bert-agreement-cpu-evaluation-v1-20261029/README.md) passed its directional and matched-distractor criteria on eight public families, but an earlier subject-state patch had a larger mean effect in every family. The [DistilGPT2 matched-dose factorial](../results/distilgpt2-copy-head-factorial-v1-20261028/README.md) found effects from two candidate heads on public prompts; the joint intervention had a larger total input dose than either single intervention, and downstream computation and the nonlinear log-probability readout can contribute to the observed interaction. The [synthetic v2 study](../results/synthetic-causal-v2-20261029/README.md) records first-changed-block hits in all 54 model/injection cells, but its known feedforward structure favors that boundary rule; its saved-data audit does not independently establish execution chronology or exposure. These findings do not establish a unique mechanism.

## What the primary literature shows

| Source | Result or contribution, in its tested setting | Implication for this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Vig et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12265), causal mediation in pretrained language models | Intervened on neurons and attention heads while studying gender-bias measures; reported sparse and interacting effects. | Treat components as candidate mediators and test interventions, rather than treating a decodable feature as evidence it is used. Do not assume effects of separate components add. |
| [Geiger et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997), causal abstractions | Aligns neural states with variables in an explicit high-level model and tests correspondence with interchange interventions. Their case study supports parts of an NLI abstraction for a BERT-based model. | State what a candidate variable is supposed to compute and make predictions for held-out swaps. A location with an effect alone does not identify the variable's meaning. |
| [Meng et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262), causal tracing and ROME | Uses corrupted inputs and restoration of internal states to investigate factual recall, then tests a weight-editing method. The paper reports results for the studied factual associations and models. | A clean-to-corrupt patch can suggest a site for further testing. Restoration is conditional on the corruption, the replacement, and the selected output metric. |
| [Wang et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593), GPT-2 small indirect object identification | Uses several interventions, including path patching, to propose a circuit and checks faithfulness, completeness, and minimality. The study finds backup heads and remaining gaps. | Test selected components both individually and jointly; a circuit that preserves task performance under one knockout need not be a complete explanation. |
| [Faumi and Sumanathilaka (2026), preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37157v1), GPT-2 and DistilGPT-2 | Reports 35.2% recipient-versus-subject accuracy for DistilGPT-2 on the authors' IOI set. The proposed repeated-name explanation is not tested; the paper does not specify an exact checkpoint revision, tokenizer version or released prompt inventory. | Treat this as context, not an independently replicated result on our pinned checkpoint. Prespecify the behavior test and its failure outcome before investigating a recipient mechanism. |
| [Zhang and Nanda (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042), patching methods and metrics | On the investigated factual-recall and circuit-discovery tasks, changing the corruption, corrupted tokens, metric, or patching window can change localization. The authors provide tentative evidence that Gaussian corruption can induce out-of-distribution behavior. | Prespecify the primary contrast and metric; report a sensitivity analysis using alternative plausible corruptions or metrics. A location map is not independent of these choices. |
| [Heimersheim and Nanda (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255), interpretation of patching | Gives guidance on clean-to-corrupt and corrupt-to-clean directions, circuit evidence, and metric pitfalls. | Test both directions where meaningful. Qualify any necessity or sufficiency language by the actual intervention, replacement, input distribution, and task. |
| [Hase et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213), localization versus editing | In their tested factual-editing setting, representation-denoising localization did not identify the best MLP layer for overriding an existing fact. | Evaluate weight-edit locations directly; an activation patch is not a guarantee that editing the corresponding parameters will be best. |
| [Hanna et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17806), circuit faithfulness | In their experiments, circuits from a gradient-based approximation could overlap known circuits yet be less faithful under their ablation test; an integrated-gradients variant improved faithfulness. | Gradient ranking can cheaply propose sites, but verify selected candidates with actual interventions and task metrics. This paper's attribution gradients are distinct from this repository's gradient reporting channel. |
| [Kramár et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00745), attribution patching | Reports two classes of false negatives in a first-order gradient approximation to activation patching and proposes a variant that addresses those failure modes in the studied settings. | Measure false negatives against direct interventions on a tractable subset; do not equate an approximation's null score with proof that a site has no effect. |
| [Miller et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08734), faithfulness metrics | Finds circuit faithfulness scores depend on ablation and other methodological choices in the investigated settings. | Report the ablation distribution and metric with the score; measure sensitivity rather than calling one score universal. |
| [Gupta et al. (2024), InterpBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14494) | Constructs semi-synthetic transformers with intended known circuits using stricter interchange-intervention training, then evaluates circuit-discovery methods. | Known-mechanism testbeds are useful, but validate the exact ground truth and assumptions of each benchmark; success there does not establish performance on natural pretrained behaviors. |

These are case-specific empirical findings, plus each paper's own methodological proposals. They do not establish a universal choice of component, corruption, metric, or model family. They also do not imply that an activation trace alone reveals a model's reasoning.

## Define the causal question before interpreting a patch

For a deterministic evaluation, let `b` and `s` specify the base and source runs, including their inputs and any injected changes; let `h_i(s)` be the source activation at a precisely named boundary `i`, and let `m` be a fixed scalar output metric. Let `F(b; do(h_i = h_i(s)))` mean the output obtained by replacing that boundary's activation during the base run. A directly measurable paired effect is

```text
delta_i(b, s) = m(F(b; do(h_i = h_i(s)))) - m(F(b)).
```

This is the effect of the **specified computational intervention** on the specified runs. To make it reproducible, specify the model and revision, base and source inputs, corruption or injected change, layer/module/boundary, tensor slice, token alignment, invocation and generation step, replacement rule, metric, and runtime settings. For repeated or stateful modules, include cache state and define whether the patch applies to exactly one invocation or a set of invocations. Use deterministic settings or a paired design with recorded randomness; treat stochastic runs as a separate protocol.

For denoising, `b` is corrupted and `s` is clean; the effect measures what the clean replacement restores in that corrupted context. For noising, `b` is clean and `s` is corrupted; the effect measures the result of that particular replacement in the clean context. Neither direction alone establishes an unrestricted necessity or sufficiency claim. A replacement can damage unrelated information or put downstream states outside their usual joint distribution, even when its value comes from a real input. An apparently null effect can reflect redundancy, compensation, masking, an ineffective corruption, or the wrong metric rather than absence of a role.

Always save the base, source, and patched metric values and their raw paired differences. If a protocol additionally reports normalized recovery `(patched - corrupt) / (clean - corrupt)`, define the sign and aggregation in advance, including a minimum absolute denominator and the fraction of excluded or undefined cases. This filtering selects on the clean–corrupt gap, so show raw effects and eligibility for the full sample alongside the ratio. A near-zero gap makes the ratio unstable; ratios below zero or above one can occur and should not be silently clipped. A single-token logit difference can be a useful task metric when the compared tokens and alignment are well defined. Accuracy, probability, and a full-output distribution metric answer different questions; each has limitations, so designate one primary metric and make others explicit sensitivity analyses.

When intervention choices are compatible and their order is specified, an interaction on the chosen metric can be measured as `m(F_ij) - m(F_i) - m(F_j) + m(F_0)`, with `F_0`, `F_i`, `F_j`, and `F_ij` denoting no, individual, and combined interventions. Hold base, source, any randomness, and each site's replacement value fixed across the four runs. A nonzero value means nonadditivity for those interventions and that metric; downstream computations or a nonlinear output metric can produce it without a direct interaction between the sites. It does not identify a unique pathway. For overlapping or causally ordered sites, explicitly specify which values are overwritten and in what order.

## Proposed first protocol: controlled changes, then held-out interventions

The following is a proposal to be versioned before a run, not an existing result or a guarantee of discovery.

1. **Fix the testbed and ground truth.** Start with a small pretrained transformer, short tokenized inputs, and a bounded set of named sites. Record immutable model/tokenizer revisions and the exact injected operation, value, location, invocation, and seed. Keep injection coordinates and labels hidden from discovery and ranking code until predictions are frozen, then allow independent scoring against the sealed ground truth. Include both no-change runs and changes at several sites or strengths, including cases where a change is attenuated downstream. An injected operation is known ground truth for the artificial change, not ground truth for the model's learned algorithm.
2. **Split before candidate search.** Split independent prompt families or examples and source–base pairs into discovery, development, and held-out sets, avoiding template or pair leakage. Select candidate sites, thresholds, metrics, and intervention strengths on the first two sets only. Freeze a machine-readable candidate, its predicted sign and magnitude or range, the intervention and eligibility rules before evaluating the held-out set. Retain rejected candidates and failures.
3. **Run localization controls.** Compare fixed sparse and dense taps with random budget-matched locations, an activation-difference magnitude ranking, and a gradient-based ranking when the required gradient exists. State the candidate sites, search costs, and data each method may use, including any clean reference run. Under a declared budget, compare measured compute or wall time and recorded bytes; count setup and candidate selection as well as confirmation, and distinguish forward from backward work. Include identical-input no-injection negatives and independent known-injection ground truth. Define false positives using unchanged runs. Specify which injected operations are representable as a discrete site or interval under each tap policy. Score top-k site recovery only for methods returning sites; for methods returning intervals, score ground-truth coverage and interval width. Report no detections, ties, errors, false positives and missed or masked injections. First observed difference is a boundary-level observation, not necessarily the originating operation, and may be absent after cancellation or attenuation; using the injection coordinate to choose or label a candidate is a sanity check, not blind localization.
4. **Test explanations, not just rankings.** On held-out pairs, run clean and corrupted baselines, self-patches at the same site as identity controls, the frozen candidate patch, and budget-matched random-site patches. Where meaningful, also test the reverse direction, plausible alternative source activations, and multiple strengths chosen before evaluation. Report the raw paired effect against baselines, the frozen prediction error and sign accuracy, failed or ineligible pairs, and a no-change/null rate. Test selected site pairs or groups when redundancy or interactions are plausible. A rescue measures a response to the specified intervention; it does not establish uniqueness.
5. **Quantify uncertainty at the right unit.** Predefine the target population and sampling structure. Prompt families and injection seeds can be crossed, so multiple independently generated injections on one prompt are not automatically independent observations. Depending on the design, resample paired prompt-family clusters or use a justified crossed/cluster method; avoid strong interval claims with very few clusters. Do not treat correlated tokens or repeated measurements from the same prompt as independent examples. Report sample counts and uncertainty, and separate exploratory multiplicity from the prespecified held-out comparisons. Save manifests, raw values, source/code hashes, seed, failures, and environment versions so a separate team can reproduce the run.

For natural pretrained-model behavior, there may be no known true circuit. Report intervention-prediction accuracy, controls, and the distribution of effects, without converting agreement with a selected site into ground-truth localization. For later model edits, freeze the edit before measuring held-out target performance and unrelated capability regressions; compare alternative locations and methods under a declared budget.
