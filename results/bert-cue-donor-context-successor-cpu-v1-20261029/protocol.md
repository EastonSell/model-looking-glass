# Same opposite cue in two donor contexts: fixed CPU protocol

## Question and cases

The preceding [signed cue-context study](https://github.com/EastonSell/model-looking-glass/blob/8b61b7513f3c4a3ff9227ef5310b4a5692c37d90/results/bert-cue-context-signed-cpu-v1-20261029/README.md) passed its class-directed criterion in two of three evaluation pairs, but none passed the stricter sensitivity rule; its active controls and same-cue donor-gap analysis remained consequential. Here we ask a different question: for the same recipient and opposite-class donor cue, how does the signed intervention effect change when that donor comes from the recipient's own lexical context versus another lexical context? The design and numerical thresholds are informed by earlier outcomes. No model output on the cases below is used to choose or tune them.

`experiments/bert_cue_donor_context_retention_cases_v1.json` fixes one discovery and three evaluation pairs, two lexical contexts per pair, and 32 full tokenizer rows before any new pretrained forward. The cues `never` and `rarely` form a negative/low-frequency group; `always` and `often` form an affirmative/high-frequency group. All texts and splits are public and visible during design, not blinded or known absent from pretraining. Compare every row with named available public BERT inputs for equality and either-direction prefix with complete and terminal-`[SEP]`-removed sequences. A collision stops the fixed attempt and is reported; no outcome-based replacement. `experiments/bert_cue_donor_context_public_exposure_v1.json` records zero relations against 1,534 row references from 28 named public sources and two fixed planned proposals; repeated rows are counted repeatedly. The separate `experiments/bert_cue_donor_context_antonym_overlap_v1.json` records zero relations with 55 additional fixed planned antonym rows. These checks cannot establish private, unknown or pretraining nonexposure.

The template `The {role} has {cue} {verb} [MASK] of the {object}.` has twelve tokens with cue, mask and object at positions 4, 6 and 9. Contexts change role, verb and object together. Those word changes, natural state distances and activation directions cannot be isolated by this design. `rarely` is scalar rather than equivalent to explicit sentential negation; cues also differ in frequency and temporal meaning. `any` has free-choice uses, and the any-minus-some metric is not a human acceptability judgment. A pass is one-sided retention, permitting arbitrarily larger alternate-context effects; it is not closeness, invariance, class specificity, or a general negation mechanism.

## Run, dose and controls

Reuse the preceding context study's `run_pair` intervention and witness path with the pinned cached `distilbert/distilbert-base-uncased` revision `12040accade4e8a0f71eabdb258fecc2e7e948be`, float32 CPU, eager attention, evaluation mode, deterministic algorithms, one intra-op and one inter-op thread, no autocast or network, and seed `2026102908`. Verify source, checkpoint, tokenizer, versions and all 32 inputs before model loading; hide CUDA before Torch import. Save CPU environment, directly waited exit and before/after state hashes.

For each pair run all eight clean variants and capture cue, object and mask after block 2 (`cue2`, `object2`, `mask2`), and cue after final block 5 (`cue5`). At each site and recipient, use four alternate-context cues then the other three cues from the recipient's own context, both directions. Within- and alternate-context donors for one matched edge have the same cue, recipient, site and requested dose. Same-cue and different-same-class donors from the alternate context, within-context same-class donors, and object/mask sites remain active comparators. Final cue5 full mask logits must equal clean bit-for-bit; clean/repeat use passive capture hooks.

Require all 224 donor/site distances finite and nonnegative, computed in coordinate order as `sqrt(fsum((float(y_i)-float(x_i)) ** 2))`. Set one pair-wide `q=min(0.5, minimum_distance)`. A tie at q=0.05 is feasible; below 0.05 save clean, distance and clean-repeat evidence without patches. At q≥0.05 run all patches even for a baseline-ineligible pair or q<0.5. Primary eligibility requires q exactly 0.5; smaller feasible q is descriptive. The cap is informed by prior studies. For each transfer use binary64 `alpha=q/distance` with `0<alpha<=1`, separately compute subtraction, multiplication and addition, then one nearest-even float32 cast. Retain recipient input, clean and donor captures, submitted and landed vectors, whole selected-site before/after states, full mask logits, q, distance, fraction and achieved norm. Require same-forward pre-state equal to clean capture, landed bits equal submitted bits, untouched token coordinates unchanged, positive actual norm and relative error ≤0.001. Self-patch and final repeat logits/captures must equal clean bit-for-bit. Failures or nonfinite data stop as incomplete with bounded witnesses where feasible.

Order pairs as listed, context A then B, cue order `never`, `rarely`, `always`, `often`, site order `cue2`, `object2`, `mask2`, `cue5`. At each site run eight self patches, then for each recipient four alternate-context cue donors and three different same-context donors in cue order; finish with eight clean repeats. Each feasible pair has 8 clean + 32 self + 224 transfer + 8 repeat = 272 forwards; q<0.05 uses 16. Maximum is 1,088 forwards. No case or condition is replaced based on outputs.

## Baseline and fixed paired outcome

Let `L=fsum((logit(any),-logit(some)))` in binary64 from float32 saved logits; each Δ is `fsum((patched_L,-recipient_clean_L))`. A pair's baseline is eligible only if all four clean `L(negative)-L(affirmative)` gaps are strictly >0.5 in both contexts. A tie fails. The screen is a selected model contrast, not grammaticality or a population sample.

For each of eight ordered recipients and both opposite-class donor cues, giving 16 matched edges, define class direction `sign=-1` for negative/low-frequency recipients and `+1` for affirmative/high-frequency recipients. Define `W=sign*Δ_within` for the opposite cue donated from the recipient's own context and `X=sign*Δ_alternate` for that **same cue** from the alternate context. Each uses `cue2`, the same recipient and q. The sign is class-directed, not guaranteed to point toward the alternate donor's own clean L; record both actual clean donor-minus-recipient gaps and every W and X.

An edge is informative iff `W>=0.02`; the boundary is inclusive. Report `W<=0` and `0<W<0.02` separately, including the raw alternate effects, rather than treating either as zero or a success/failure. For an informative edge, the fixed retention condition is `X>=0.5*W`, with the multiplication performed in binary64 and the comparison inclusive; compute the descriptive difference as `fsum((X,-0.5*W))`. This direct inequality avoids division by near-zero W and accepts effects larger than W. A stricter, separately reported sensitivity condition is `X>=W`, also inclusive. Both conditions use the identical informative-edge set.

A pair is evaluable only if baseline passes, q=0.5 and at least 12 of its 16 edges are informative. If evaluable, it passes the primary iff at least 12 **informative** edges pass the retention inequality; otherwise it fails. The sensitivity uses the same eligibility and at least 12 informative edges passing its own inequality. Report informative count, passing count, passing/16 and passing/informative, all 16 edges and their reasons. Thus the required passing proportion among informative edges ranges from 12/12 to 12/16. The 0.02, half-size and 12-edge thresholds are operational, not significance levels. Conditioning on W selects by an intervention outcome and can change the evaluated set; it is not a clean-baseline screen.

Discovery is separate and never enters aggregation. The evaluation aggregate is `unevaluable` if fewer than two of its three pairs are evaluable. Otherwise it is `pass` only if at least two **evaluable evaluation pairs** pass, and `fail` otherwise. In particular, two evaluable pairs with one pass and one fail produce aggregate failure, while the third remains unevaluable rather than an observed negative. Apply the same rule to sensitivity. Report all pair outcomes and denominators; no imputation, population rate or post-output retuning. Edges share recipients, donor contexts and reciprocal directions, so they are dependent.

For each edge also retain both natural distances, signed clean donor−recipient gaps, same-cue and different-same-class alternate-context effects, within-context same-class effects, and matched actual doses. Report the cosine of the two clean donor-minus-recipient vectors at cue2: form each coordinate subtraction in binary64, use `fsum` for the binary64 dot product, the fixed distance formula above for both norms, and divide dot by their binary64 product. If either norm is zero, report null. This describes direction geometry; equal L2 norms do not imply equal functional impact and cosine does not establish semantic identity. Geometry and achieved-dose tables are reconstructed from the retained clean/transfer witnesses by a fixed model-free reader.
