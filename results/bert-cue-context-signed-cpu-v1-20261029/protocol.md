# Signed cue-context transfer: prospective CPU protocol

## Question and fixed inputs

In the preceding [cue-context study](https://github.com/EastonSell/model-looking-glass/blob/9c16f0590532a8cfe7aa95a3dc249c74adc36492/results/bert-cue-context-transfer-cpu-v1-20261029/README.md), same-cue alternate-context transfers met the fixed half-magnitude rule against matched same-class transfers in both evaluation pairs, while five of sixteen recipient effects had signs opposite to the same-class comparator. That magnitude result did not establish a direction or a cue representation. On those already observed pairs, a different signed contrast was positive for all sixteen recipients; the design below was selected after seeing those outcomes and is not a blinded replication of that exploratory observation.

The new public manifest `experiments/bert_cue_context_signed_cases_v1.json` fixes one discovery and three evaluation pairs, each with two lexical contexts and the four cues `never`, `rarely`, `always`, `often`. The first two are negative/low-frequency cues and the others affirmative/high-frequency cues. All 32 texts, token IDs, attention masks, pairings, splits and positions are fixed before a new pretrained forward. Discovery and evaluation texts are visible during design; they are not used for post-output tuning, and absence from pretraining is unknown. Compare every fixed row with the named available public BERT input inventories, both complete and with a terminal `[SEP]` removed, for equality and either-direction prefixes. Any collision stops the fixed attempt and is reported; no output-based case replacement. The independent receipt `experiments/bert_cue_context_signed_public_exposure_v1.json` records zero relations across 1,287 row references in 27 named public or fixed planned snapshots; a second, `experiments/bert_cue_context_signed_public_overlap_independent_v1.json`, reports zero across 1,324 references in 28 named public files. Repeated rows across snapshots are counted repeatedly. These checks are limited to those named inputs, not private or unknown prior exposure.

Use the same 12-token template and cue, mask and object positions 4, 6 and 9 as the prior context study. Context pairs change role, verb and object together, so their effects cannot be assigned to one word, syntax, or discourse feature. `rarely` is a scalar frequency cue rather than equivalent to explicit sentential negation; the groups differ in lexical, frequency and temporal meaning. `any` can have free-choice uses. The two-logit metric below is not a human acceptability test, and neither a pass nor a fail identifies a general negation mechanism. The inference is limited to these public, nonblind, correlated cases and the fixed model/runtime.

## Model, interventions and retained evidence

Reuse the preceding study's exact `run_pair` observation and intervention schedule with cached `distilbert/distilbert-base-uncased` revision `12040accade4e8a0f71eabdb258fecc2e7e948be` on float32 CPU, eager attention, one intra-op and one inter-op thread, deterministic algorithms, no network or autocast, and seed `2026102907`. Verify exact source, pinned checkpoint/tokenizer and versions before model loading, hide CUDA before importing Torch, and retain CPU environment, before/after model-state hashes and the directly waited process exit.

For each pair run eight clean variants, capture cue, object and mask after block 2 (`cue2`, `object2`, `mask2`) and cue after final block 5 (`cue5`). For each of eight recipients at each site, use the four cues in the alternate context then the three different cues in the recipient's own context in fixed cue order; retain both directions. Thus the two opposite-class donors, same-cue donor and different-same-class donor for a recipient share that alternate donor context, recipient, site and requested norm. Also retain all within-context donors. The object and mask controls are active comparators, not semantic nulls. Final-cue transfers must preserve the full clean mask-logit vector bit-for-bit.

Calculate all 224 donor distances per pair as `sqrt(fsum((float(y_i)-float(x_i)) ** 2))` in coordinate order. Every value must be finite and nonnegative; otherwise stop incomplete. Set the common requested norm `q=min(0.5, min(all 224 distances))`. If q<0.05, save clean and final repeat evidence but no patches; a tie at 0.05 is feasible. For q≥0.05 run every prescribed patch even if the baseline fails or q<0.5. Only q=0.5 is eligible for either fixed outcome below; smaller positive feasible doses are retained descriptively. The 0.5 cap was selected with knowledge of the prior studies. Equal requested input norms do not make directions or functional impacts equal.

Use binary64 `alpha=q/distance` with `0<alpha<=1`, separate binary64 subtraction, multiplication and addition and one nearest-even float32 cast. Save recipient input, clean and donor captures, submitted and landed vectors, full selected-site before/after states and full mask logits, q, distance, fraction and achieved norm. Same-forward pre-state must equal clean capture, landed bits must equal submitted bits, unselected coordinates must remain unchanged, and achieved norm must be positive with relative error ≤0.001. Self-patch and final unpatched repeat outputs and captures must equal clean bit-for-bit. A control, nonfinite, landing or partial-forward failure invalidates the attempt; preserve bounded returned witnesses and a named phase where feasible. Clean and repeat use passive capture hooks.

Order is pair as listed, context A then B, cue `never`, `rarely`, `always`, `often`, and site `cue2`, `object2`, `mask2`, `cue5`. At each site run all eight self patches, then seven donors per recipient in the fixed order above, then after all sites repeat eight clean forwards. A feasible pair uses 8 clean + 32 self + 224 transfer + 8 repeat = 272 forwards; a q<0.05 pair uses 16. Four pairs require at most 1,088 forwards. Record all baselines, distances, effects, failures and ineligible cases, without substitution.

## Baseline and signed primary

Let `L=logit(any)-logit(some)` using binary64 `fsum` of the two saved float32 logits, and define each transfer effect Δ as `fsum((patched_L,-recipient_clean_L))`. The pair is baseline-eligible only when all four clean `L(negative)-L(affirmative)` gaps are strictly >0.5 in **each** context. A baseline tie fails. This outcome-selected model-margin screen is not a population or grammaticality screen.

For every ordered recipient at `cue2`, let `s` be the same-cue effect from the alternate context, `c` the other same-class cue effect from that context, and `o1,o2` both opposite-class cue effects from it. Set class direction `sign=-1` for a negative/low-frequency recipient and `sign=+1` for an affirmative/high-frequency recipient. Compute, with binary64 `fsum`, `D=min(sign*o1,sign*o2)-max(abs(s),abs(c))`. Both opposite donors must individually exceed **both** absolute controls: a pair passes iff every one of its eight D values is strictly >0; ties fail. This is a class-directed output test, not automatically a movement toward the actual donor's clean L. Save each raw signed effect, every class-oriented opposite effect, both control magnitudes, the exact limiting donor/control and ties, and the actual clean donor-minus-recipient L for both opposite donors and the same-class donor, including discordant clean-gap signs.

The discovery pair is always separate. Each evaluation pair is primary-eligible iff its baseline passes and q=0.5; report primary D and its strict decision whenever patches exist, even if the pair is ineligible. At least two of the three evaluation pairs must be eligible; otherwise the aggregate is `unevaluable`. Given at least two eligible pairs, aggregate `pass` requires at least two passes **among the eligible evaluation pairs**; otherwise `fail`. A descriptive raw pass in an ineligible pair never contributes to the numerator. Report all pair statuses, eligible and passing denominators and all individual values. No outcome is imputed or retuned. This 2-of-3 descriptive rule and zero boundary are operational, not calibrated statistical significance.

Prespecify a stricter sensitivity analysis with the same pair eligibility and aggregate, counting only eligible evaluation passes, but require every D strictly >0.05. A tie at 0.05 fails. These two analyses do not establish an invariant cue representation, because the same-class and same-cue interventions are active controls and donor state geometry and lexical context still differ.

## Secondary same-cue donor-gap analysis

For each recipient let `G=fsum((alternate_same_cue_clean_L,-recipient_clean_L))`, and when `abs(G)>=0.5` define `R=sign(G)*s` and `K=fsum((R,-abs(c)))`. A tie at the absolute-gap floor is included; zero cannot meet the floor. Retain every G, s, c, R and K where defined, and both directions. The two directions for each cue have opposite G and equal absolute G, so six qualified recipients ordinarily means at least three qualifying cue pairs, not six independent observations; report the four cue-level gaps and both directions. Report counts of R>0 (toward the donor's clean score) and K>0 (toward it by more than the matched same-class absolute shift), with exact qualified denominators; ties are not positive. Individual gap-ineligible recipients are kept, not imputed. Whenever patches exist also report these raw counts even for an unevaluable pair: zero qualified recipients gives raw counts zero and null means. A pair's secondary summary is evaluable only if baseline passes, q=0.5, and at least six of its eight recipients meet the gap floor. The q=0.5 screen holds requested dose common across these comparisons; smaller q can still be a valid intervention and remains descriptive. Otherwise retain its individual observations and label its summary unevaluable. Show per-pair counts, eligible denominators and mean R and K over qualified recipients in fixed order, and a descriptive evaluation pooled count/denominator over evaluable pairs; if none are evaluable the pooled summary is unevaluable. Do not assign a binary secondary success criterion. This direction and screen depend on clean L from whole sentences, so donorward alignment does not imply portable cue information.

## Full vocabulary and execution limits

Save all full mask logits, top-five tokens with token-ID tie order, target logits and stable log probabilities. For every transfer compute full-vocabulary TV and TV after deleting `any` and `some` and renormalizing. Use binary64 max-shifted exponentials, `fsum` in vocabulary order, and half the absolute probability-difference sum. Retain clean and patched `any`+`some` mass so the excluded-TV residual mass is visible. These are descriptive outcomes, not semantic specificity criteria. Report individual donor effects, TV, changes in top token and active-position controls.

Freeze this protocol, manifest, exact executable and independently reviewed decision/control method before the first pretrained forward. Store sync-flushed gzip JSONL on E: with at most 2 MiB per serialized event and 512 MiB compressed total. Require at least 2 GiB free on the output volume and 1 GiB on the source volume before launch, then 1 GiB on both before every event; these are observations, not reservations. Record incomplete attempts and do not replace inputs or retry based on outcomes. Independently replay saved identities, schedule, full-logit controls, dose geometry, landings, baselines, signed rules and all ineligible cases. Saved source and records do not independently attest execution or pretraining history.
